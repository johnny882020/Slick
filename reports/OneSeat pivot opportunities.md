# Sell the defensible file, not the agent

The best opportunity for a 2-5 engineer team that already has this engine is an **anti-money-laundering (AML) "inspection-ready file" desk for UK conveyancers, small law firms and estate agents**. It would then extend to EU firms covered by the EU AML Regulation, which applies from **10 July 2027**. The runner-up is a **multi-marketplace EU product-compliance keeper** for small online sellers, covering product safety (GPSR), packaging (EPR/PPWR) and battery rules. Close behind is a **US HVAC utility-rebate and warranty claims desk**. All three share one pattern. The money is lost when a third party (a regulator, a marketplace or a utility administrator) penalises, delists or refuses to pay because a file is incomplete or not reasoned. The rules are local and tied to dates. No big AI suite or well-funded startup owns any of these jobs. "A human approves before it acts" no longer sets a product apart, because Anthropic, Shopify, Square, Toast and Xero all now advertise it. The pitch has to be the evidence record: data kept on the customer's machine, a time-stamped audit trail an inspector will accept, and rule packs per country or programme. On pricing, people pay most in three ways: a per-matter fee the firm passes on to its client, a 20-35% share of money recovered, or a flat fee of $250+ a month set against a named service or hire. Tools under $50 a month keep only about a quarter of their revenue. Everything here comes from desk research. Reddit and most review sites were blocked, many figures are sponsored by vendors, and willingness to pay is inferred rather than observed. So the report ends with a six-week validation plan: interviews, paid pilots, and kill criteria written down in advance.

## Approval gates became table stakes in 2026

Generic back-office work for small businesses is now bundled or free. Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business on 13 May 2026. It ships 15 workflows, including an invoice chaser, a month-end prepper and a payroll planner, and the user approves "before anything is sent, posted or paid" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-small-business)). The other large suites charge about the same per user per month: **ChatGPT Business about $20-25** ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-work-review)), Microsoft 365 Copilot Business **$21** ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2025/12/02/microsoft-365-copilot-business-the-future-of-work-for-small-businesses/)) and Gemini Enterprise Business from **$21 a seat** ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise/business)). Intuit's QuickBooks agents send invoices, track payments and reconcile books "at no additional cost" ([QuickBooks AU](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/au/blog/accountants-and-bookkeepers/intuit-intelligence-ias/)). Xero's JAX "automatically reconciles books and chases invoices" ([Xero](https://blog.xero.com/product-updates/xerocon-london-2026-agentic-era-small-business-finance/)). Shopify's Sidekick stages bulk changes after a single plan approval ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/uk/editions/fall2026)). Square's Squaremates proposes actions for the owner to approve ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/square-launches-ai-agent-to-help-restaurants-run-their-businesses/)). This independently confirms the founder's rejections: a generic SMB agent, invoice chasing and month-end prep are now features inside products small businesses already pay for. It also means the current product's core promise, an approval gate on the user's own model key, is copied by every incumbent.

General agents still fail in predictable places, and those places point to the opening. On the Remote Labor Index, the best model fully automated **2.5% of real freelance projects** in October 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26787v1)). Secondary coverage reports about **16%** by July 2026, a figure not verified on the leaderboard ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-can-now-complete-16-percent-of-freelance-jobs-at-pro-quality-up-from-2-5-percent-eight-months-ago/)). OpenAI's agent pauses at logins and hands the browser back to the user ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)). Half of small-business owners named data security as their biggest hesitation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-small-business)). The FTC's DoNotPay order bars claims of matching a lawyer without evidence ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/DoNotPayInc-APCOAPC.pdf)). Launches are US-first, and Intuit's agents work only in English and French ([QuickBooks CA](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/ca/resources/accountants/your-new-more-powerful-quickbooks/)). Phone work, once a possible niche, is now contested from both ends. Consumer agents such as Google's Ask for Me call businesses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/30/googles-ask-for-me-feature-calls-businesses-on-your-behalf-to-inquire-about-services-pricing/)). Avoca raised over $125M at a $1B valuation to answer phones for trades ([Avoca](https://www.avoca.ai/blog/avoca-raises-125m-series-b-1b-valuation)). Jobber bundles an AI receptionist for $29 a month and ServiceTitan charges $2.75 per call ([levelnex](https://www.levelnex.ai/insights/fsm-ai-receptionist-transparency)).

**Inference:** what the engine offers that the suites do not is everything around the approval step. Each item maps to a specific buyer problem. **Local data storage** answers German professional-secrecy law (§ 203 StGB). Under it, a GDPR processing agreement does not cover professional secrecy, and "providers outside the EU generally do not give such an undertaking" ([IWW](https://www.iww.de/ak/digitalisierung/berufsgeheimnis-und-workflows-so-starten-sie-mit-ki-ohne-das-mandantengeheimnis-zu-riskieren-f176412)). An **audit log of every action** becomes the "time-stamped, auditable verification event" that the EU AML regime expects, according to vendor commentary ([Zealid](https://www.zealid.com/en/blog/what-amlr-actually-requires-from-small-firms-before-july-2027)). **Skills as files** can hold one rule pack per country, marketplace or utility programme. **Sandboxed scripts** can parse bank statements, nameplate photos and supplier PDFs. **MCP connectors** can reach several systems that no single platform sees. The product should be sold as "we produce X to a known standard and can prove it", not as an agent that can do anything.

### Top pick: an AML file desk that writes the reasoning regulators inspect

**Evidence.** The UK Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) publishes better data on this pain than most vendors publish on theirs. Its 2025-26 AML report rated 937 firms: **22% compliant, 56% partially compliant and 21.9% not compliant**. Thirty-six percent had ineffective client and matter risk assessments (CMRAs), and missing CMRAs were the main reason **42% of non-compliant firms** were referred. Twenty percent of files lacked evidence of screening for politically exposed persons (PEPs) and 21% lacked evidence of sanctions screening. There were 111 reports for failed source-of-funds checks. The SRA concluded 145 settlement agreements totalling **£2,003,759**, which averages about £13,800 each by our arithmetic, plus adjudicator and tribunal fines. It supervises 5,422 firms ([SRA](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/aml-annual-report-2025-26/)). The regulator explicitly criticises "overly simplistic, template-based CMRA forms" ([Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/blog/the-sras-2025-aml-report-what-law-firms-need-to-know)). In other words, it penalises the very thing a form tool produces. Estate agents face a parallel regime: HMRC issued 170 penalties totalling **£835,842** to estate agency businesses in six months ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/estate-agents-fined-over-800k-for-aml-breaches/)). Source-of-funds work is manual. Firms request three months of bank statements, completion statements and gift declarations ([Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/how-to-streamline-client-onboarding-with-advanced-source-of-funds-checks)). The money already flows per matter: Thirdfort lists ID checks at £15 and suggests firms recharge clients £15-30 ([Thirdfort](https://support.thirdfort.com/hc/en-gb/articles/28359965339805-Can-I-pass-on-the-cost-of-a-Thirdfort-check-to-a-Client)). The EU AML Regulation (AMLR) applies directly from **10 July 2027** to law firms, notaries, tax advisers and estate agents ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/what-is-the-new-eu-aml-regime)). KPMG expects a "long tail of medium, small and even micro firms" to need major process changes ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/risk-and-regulation/kpmg-anti-money-laundering-authority-office-advisory-services/amlr-for-non-financial-firms-real-estate.html)). The detailed customer due diligence (CDD) standards are still being written during 2026-27 ([PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/from-action-to-acceleration-amlas-single-programming-document-for-2026-2028-charts-the-course-ahead)). Small law firms are already wary of AI that acts on its own: Clio users complain on Trustpilot that the vendor "automatically enrolled our firm" in its AI platform ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.clio.com)), and **55-57%** of small firms and solos have no AI policy ([Clio](https://www.clio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-Solo-and-Small-Legal-Trends-Report.pdf)).

**Inference.** The product takes in the outputs of existing ID and source-of-funds tools (Thirdfort or Amiqus reports, bank-statement PDFs, client declarations). It reconciles transactions against the declared funding, flags unexplained credits and gifts, and writes a reasoned CMRA from SRA and Law Society guidance encoded as skill files. It drafts any client questions for the fee earner to approve. The money-laundering reporting officer (MLRO) signs off, and the result is a tamper-evident file that can be exported when an inspector asks to "show me the file". Firm-wide risk assessments and policies are kept current alongside. Every element of the engine maps to something the regulator inspects. It also needs no deep system integration, which makes a 3-month MVP realistic for UK conveyancing alone. German, Dutch and French rule packs would follow for AMLR.

It ranks first for four reasons. The buyer is a professional under regulatory compulsion. The fee is already passed through to the end client. Regulatory obligations recur with every new matter, which should retain better than discretionary tools. And the market grows by an order of magnitude in July 2027, in local-language rule sets that US vendors underserve.

Three risks need checking first. First, the desk research did not confirm whether Thirdfort, Amiqus or Credas already generate reasoned CMRAs. If they do, the gap shrinks to integration. Second, a missed flag creates liability. That argues for keeping the fee earner and MLRO as the decision makers and never claiming to replace them, which is the DoNotPay lesson. Third, the product must stay centred on the reasoning record. Client questions about source of funds are a small step inside the file, not the product. If the product drifts into chasing clients for documents, it becomes a thin variant of the document-chasing desk the founder rejected. It is also not a supplier compliance questionnaire: the counterparty is a statutory supervisor inspecting the firm's own judgement, not a customer's procurement form.

### Runner-up: keeping EU listings live across marketplaces

**Evidence.** EU product and packaging compliance became a condition for listing during 2025-26, and the marketplaces now enforce it. An Amazon-commissioned study found **64 unique registration fields across 10 EU markets**, 55% of them country-specific, and registration taking 2-6 weeks per country ([About Amazon EU](https://www.aboutamazon.eu/news/policy/new-study-why-small-businesses-struggle-with-eu-packaging-rules-and-how-to-fix-it)). The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) has applied since **12 August 2026**. It requires an authorised representative (AR) in each country where a seller is not established. The Council closed the deferral file in June 2026 without a deferral ([EXPANDO](https://www.expan.do/post/ppwr-marketplaces-authorised-representative-enforcement)). bol.com began taking product groups offline from **16 September 2026** when legally required product information was missing ([bol.com](https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/nadp/legally-required-product-information)). A vendor reports that Zalando will block assortments without packaging-EPR details from 1 January 2027 ([ChannelEngine](https://www.channelengine.com/nl/blog/packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr)). Batteries, including those inside electronics, have needed EPR registration in every member state since August 2025 ([Zalando](https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/what-is-the-eu-epr-battery-regulation)). Some Etsy sellers simply left the EU, calling compliance "literally impossible" ([Etsy Community](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Technical-Issues/EU-amp-NI-GPSR-block-for-Digital-Sales/m-p/147230486)). Sellers already pay for pieces of this. A GPSR responsible-person service quote ran from €650 to €2,000 a year ([Amazon EU forum](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/d2c4b0b5-7e83-4d6d-9dd6-2d6254a65375)). VAT and EPR services cost €39-49 per country per month ([hellotax](https://hellotax.com/pricing)). Amazon's EPR "Pay on Behalf" serves **over 300,000 sellers**, but it covers Amazon sales only ([About Amazon EU](https://www.aboutamazon.eu/news/policy/new-study-why-small-businesses-struggle-with-eu-packaging-rules-and-how-to-fix-it)). A competitor reports that Taxdoo discontinued its managed VAT service in April 2026, which hints at thin margins in human-heavy compliance bureaus ([hellotax](https://hellotax.com/blog/eu-vat-compliance-providers/)).

**Inference.** No vendor found keeps a seller's compliance state consistent across Amazon EU, bol, Kaufland, Allegro, Zalando, Etsy, eBay and the seller's own shop. Each marketplace checks only its own listings, and none has an incentive to reconcile registrations across its rivals. The MVP would do six things. It ingests the catalogue through the Shopify or WooCommerce APIs, Amazon's Selling Partner API (SP-API), and the eBay and bol APIs. It scans each SKU, country and marketplace for gaps. It extracts manufacturer and safety data from supplier documents stored locally. It drafts GPSR safety text in each language. It produces registration data packs for each national register. And it pushes fixes to listings only after approval, logging each one.

The team cannot legally act as the AR or responsible person, so it would partner with AR providers. National registers probably lack APIs (unverified), so a human submits. This ranks second, not first. The buyer pool is larger and the forcing dates are sharper. But small sellers are price-sensitive, and **38% of marketplace sellers describe themselves as "distressed"** ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/the-marketplace-pulse-seller-index-results-2026)). The likely price of €49-199 a month sits in the retention band where AI products keep less than half their revenue (see the pricing section). An account-health and appeal module (opportunity 5) would share the same document vault and could raise revenue per account later. Like the AML desk, this is not a supplier questionnaire: the counterparty is a marketplace or national register, and the penalty is a listing going offline.

### Third: the HVAC rebate and warranty desk, the least contested and most thinly evidenced

**Evidence.** The 25C federal tax credit ended on 31 December 2025, so utilities now pay most heat-pump incentives, and they pay them to the contractor. At SMUD (Sacramento's municipal utility), rebates run $2,000-3,000 plus a $2,000 panel bonus. Contractors who front the rebate to the customer carry **$3,000-5,000 per job**. More than 100 projects at one administrator went unpaid because the signed application was mailed instead of uploaded. AHRI certificate numbers that did not yet resolve in the AHRI directory cost one contractor three weeks. Sendbacks are rising, and repeated sendbacks trigger mandatory mentoring. SMUD alone targets 4,870 conversions in 2026 ([ACHR News](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/166897-life-after-the-federal-credit-running-utility-rebates-as-an-operating-process)). Warranty work is similar. Contractors charge customers $50-125 per claim just to recover admin time ([Rescue Heat and Air](https://rescueheatandair.com/blog/can-hvac-companies-charge-warranty-fee/)), and distributors employ dedicated warranty-claims processors ([Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/ambiententerprises/jobs/4945852008)).

**Inference.** No software vendor focused on rebate packet assembly or AHRI matching turned up. That gives this the highest incumbent-gap score of any candidate. But the absence may reflect search limits rather than an empty market. The pain rests mainly on one contractor's trade-press article. Funding is political: California's HEEHRA single-family funds were fully reserved or waitlisted in 2026 ([servicemag](https://servicemag.org/news/california-heehra-heat-pump-rebates-fully-waitlisted)). Expansion is linear, one programme at a time, and manufacturer warranty portals may block automation. It is still worth validating in parallel, for three reasons. It needs no telephony and carries almost no legal risk. Every accepted packet is a visible dollar outcome. And it hedges the top two with a US, non-regulatory segment. EU heat-pump subsidy schemes (Germany's BEG/KfW, France's MaPrimeRénov', the UK Boiler Upgrade Scheme) are plausible analogues that this research did not cover.

### The rest of the ranking, and the ideas left out

**Therapy and PT audit readiness** (#4) has the largest loss per incident. Payers audit 3-6 years back, sample about 50 claims and extrapolate the error rate to thousands ([Legal 500 / BIR](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/food-drugs-healthcare-life-sciences/multi-year-insurance-audits-of-physical-therapy-practices-documentation-medical-necessity-and-the-growing-use-of-extrapolation)). Coverage of 2026 data describes insurers that "deny less just to claw back more later" ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366651012/Insurers-deny-less-just-to-claw-back-more-later-analysis)). Against that, it carries HIPAA overhead, needs clinical-billing expertise, and audits are rare for any single practice.

**Marketplace account-health appeals** (#5) has strong per-case willingness to pay. Nothing found shows Amazon's free Seller Assistant drafting or filing appeals ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/amazon-launches-ai-agent-to-help-sellers-complete-tasks-and-manage-their-businesses)). But suspensions are episodic, so it works better as a module of #2 than as a standalone product.

**Health-claim appeals for households** (#6) has the best pain data in the whole study. In 2025, 21% of privately insured adults reported a denial affecting themselves or a family member ([Commonwealth Fund](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/surveys/2026/jun/how-health-insurance-coverage-denials-affect-americans-2025-affordability-survey)). But only about 2% of US households pay for AI services, per secondary coverage of a16z ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/us-households-pay-ai-services-a16z/)), and free letter tools set the price.

**Scope Guard** (#7) is the best engine fit among the solo-professional ideas. Its pain figures are unverified, and its price would land in the worst-retaining band. It ties for fourth on the unweighted scale (25/35 alongside #5) and falls to seventh once pain and money count double. That shows its appeal is engine fit, not money.

**Roofing supplements** (#8) carries real money per claim but faces early AI entrants (RoofGenius, RAFTER AI, SupplementSnap) and the Leap-Verisk integration ([Leap](https://leaptodigital.com/blog/leap-teams-up-with-verisk-to-bring-xactimate-into-insurance-roofing/)). It also raises an unverified public-adjuster licensing question.

Several other ideas were considered and left out. A Medicaid long-term-care packet builder sold to elder-law firms deserves a later look but has thinner evidence. An executor estate-admin workspace runs into Empathy's $72M Series C ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/05/29/exclusive-empathy-raises-72-million-series-c-to-tackle-the-agonizing-logistics-of-death/)). Insurance-agency renewal review is now crowded with funded tools such as Outmarket ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/05/14/outmarket-ai-bags-17m-to-expand-insurance-platform/)). UK landlord compliance reminders are cheap and plentiful. FBA hard-claims recovery is a shrinking pool now that Amazon reimburses at manufacturing cost ([Value Added Resource](https://valueaddedresource.net/amazon-fba-inventory-reimbursement-2025)). And an EU flight-claim self-filer is a small ticket with EU261 reform pending.

## Money leaks wherever a third party demands proof

Affinity-mapping the six research files produces five clusters that recur across segments. Their combined message is that small operators rarely lose money for lack of drafting help, which is now free. They lose it at the hand-off to a party that can refuse, penalise or claw back.

### Contingency, pass-through and named-hire prices beat seats

Pricing models with the highest observed willingness to pay share one feature: the price is tied to money the buyer would otherwise lose. Contingency on recovered money clusters at **20-35%**. Chargeflow takes 25% of recovered chargebacks ([Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing)) and Getida 25% of FBA reimbursements ([RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/getida-review/)). Ownwell takes 25-35% of property-tax savings ([AppealDesk](https://www.appealdesk.com/blog/ownwell-pricing-review)) and AirHelp 35% of flight compensation ([AirHelp](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/our-fees)). Rocket Money takes 35-60% of a negotiated bill's first-year savings ([Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge)).

Contingency creates backlash and invites flat-fee competition. A Trustpilot reviewer calls Getida "too expensive (25% fee) for a poor service" ([Trustpilot](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/getida.com)). Counties point out that property-tax appeals can be filed free ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/2025/07/some-companies-offer-paid-help-with-taxes-county-officials-say-its-free)). Rocket Money has 273 BBB complaints in three years ([BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/md/silver-spring/profile/billing-services/rocket-money-inc-0241-236043013/complaints?page=13)). AirHelp now sells a €39.99 annual membership that removes its percentage fee, and ChargePay offers chargeback defence from $19.99 a month ([AirHelp](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/our-fees); [ChargePay](https://apps.shopify.com/chargepay)).

Flat fees set against a named service or hire cluster at **$150-700 a month**. Smith.ai's AI receptionist starts at $150 and Sameday at $449 ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/8-best-ai-receptionists-for-home-services-in-2026/)). Toast IQ Grow costs $699 and is positioned against hiring a marketer ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). Eve is estimated at about $500 per attorney ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/eve)). Per-matter pass-through, as with Thirdfort's £15-30 recharge, is the regulated-practice version of the same logic. Per-outcome pricing holds only for high-value outcomes. Commodity outcomes collapse: HubSpot halved its price to $0.50 per resolved conversation ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/hubspots-customer-agent-and-prospecting-agent-now-you-pay-when-the-task-is-complete)), and Intercom Fin fell from $1.90 to $0.99 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/intercom)).

The retention evidence on cheap tools is the strongest warning in the study. ChartMogul's data on about 200 AI-native companies shows the following, though it is directional only ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/)):

| Price per month | Gross revenue retention (GRR) | Net revenue retention (NRR) |
|---|---|---|
| Above $250 | 70% | 85% |
| $50-249 | 45% | 61% |
| Below $50 | 23% | 32% |

Mobile AI apps retain 21.1% annually against 30.7% for non-AI apps ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ai-app-retention-study)). Freed's growth slowed from +66% to +22% as scribes became free ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/freed/)). The inference for this team: price at or above about $250 a month per firm, or per matter or per accepted outcome. Do not launch a $29 seat.

## What this evidence can and cannot tell you

This is desk research only. No customer was interviewed, no price was tested, and no prototype was shown to anyone. Reddit could not be read: both search and fetch refused reddit.com in every research thread. Capterra, G2 and GetApp pages returned HTTP 403, and EcommerceBytes has stopped publishing ([EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2025/06/29/whats-worse-than-lost-fba-inventory-when-amazon-finds-it/)). User voice therefore comes from Trustpilot, Amazon, Shopify and Etsy forums, and trade press. That user voice skews toward people angry enough to post.

Weigh the evidence in four tiers. Regulator and official data are the most reliable: the SRA, HMRC, KFF, the Commonwealth Fund, the AMA, the EU's customs directorate (DG TAXUD) and bol's own partner rules. Trade-press reports from a single named practitioner come next, such as the ACHR rebate article that carries most of opportunity 3. Vendor-sponsored figures should be treated as directional. They include the Amazon-commissioned EPR study, the Chargebacks911 field report, the MBO Partners and Upwork freelancer surveys, every missed-call statistic, and every scope-creep statistic. One audit of a vendor's missed-call page found its own revenue table did not add up ([CallJolt](https://calljolt.com/blog/guides/home-service-missed-call-statistics)). Single-sourced secondary claims should be held loosely, such as the 16% Remote Labor Index score and a reported Salesforce purchase of Fin.

Some things in this report are inferences, not cited facts. Every score in the ranking table is an inference. So is every fee model and price point in the opportunity sections, and so is the claim that no vendor serves rebate packets or multi-marketplace compliance, which may simply reflect search limits. So is the assumption that professional buyers churn less than micro-SMBs, and so is the assumption that local data storage will change buying decisions rather than just survey answers.

Several facts that would move the ranking are still unknown:

- whether Thirdfort, Amiqus or Credas already produce reasoned CMRAs;
- whether national EPR registers and HVAC manufacturer warranty portals allow automated submission;
- what customer acquisition costs look like in any of these segments, since no source reported them;
- whether the EU AMLR's simplified regime for very small firms reduces demand among micro-practices ([Ecovis](https://global.ecovis.com/?p=19986)).

## Six weeks of user research before writing code

### Research objectives

The research should answer five questions for each of the top three opportunities, using evidence from past behaviour:

1. Did the costly failure actually happen to this buyer recently? That means a regulator or file-review finding, a blocked listing, or a rebate sendback.
2. What did it cost in hours, fines, lost sales or capital tied up?
3. What does the buyer spend today on tools, services and staff to prevent it?
4. Who signs off on buying a fix, and how did they buy the last comparable tool?
5. Will at least a few buyers pay for the outcome before the product exists?

A secondary objective is to test whether local data storage and an inspectable audit trail change the conversation. The founder needs to know whether they are a buying reason or merely nice to hear.

### Method

Run **5-8 interviews per segment**, so 15-24 in total. Each is a 50-minute remote session with an interviewer and a note-taker, recorded with consent. The guides below follow the requested five-block structure. Every question asks about a specific past instance ("the last time…"). Participants should screen-share redacted artefacts where possible, such as a CMRA template, a delisting notice or a sendback email, because artefacts are more reliable than recollection. Nothing is pitched until block four. Debrief within 24 hours of each interview and keep a running affinity map using the same clusters as this report, so that new evidence either confirms or breaks them.

In parallel, run one **concierge or pre-sale test per segment**. The team delivers the outcome manually with the existing engine, and the customer pays.

- **AML:** a paid four-week pilot for 3-5 firms covering 10 live matters each, at roughly £250 per firm. The engine runs on the firm's own machine and the MLRO signs off each file.
- **EU compliance:** a paid one-off "listing compliance gap report" at about €99-249. It covers each SKU, country and marketplace, built from the seller's catalogue export, and ends with an offer of ongoing monitoring.
- **HVAC:** preparing each shop's next 10 rebate or warranty packets at $25-50 per accepted packet, with the shop's own office submitting them.

Prices are starting points chosen to sit near the anchors above. The aim is to observe payment, not to optimise price.

## Conclusion

The pivot that the evidence supports is a change in what the company sells, not a new agent. It should sell the record that a sceptical third party will accept. The engine's parts already have buyers once they are framed this way: the approval queue, the audit log, local storage, rule files and document-parsing scripts. A UK compliance officer needs a reasoned file the SRA will not call "template-based". An EU seller needs proof that every listing carries the right registration in every country. An HVAC office needs a packet the administrator will not send back. None of these buyers is short of drafting help, and all of them already pay someone (a check provider, a compliance bureau, an office manager) to avoid the loss.

The top three also share most of their build: a local evidence vault, per-jurisdiction rule packs, a pre-submission validator, an approval queue and an inspector-ready export. The six-week research can therefore choose the segment without forcing a rewrite. Two facts would change the ranking fastest. If existing AML-check vendors already draft reasoned risk assessments, the top pick weakens to an integration play. If sellers will pay for a one-off gap report, the runner-up moves up, because its buyer pool and forcing dates are larger. Those two checks belong in week one.
